01Scaling a product sales campaignMeta Ads · Ecommerce · 10 March–8 April 2026+

My role

I created and managed the campaign, including the budget allocation and creative updates.

Approach

I kept the campaign structure that was working and focused changes on where the budget went and what people saw.

02Broad sales and remarketingMeta Ads · Ecommerce product drop+

Goal

Build sales volume while bringing interested visitors back to complete their purchase.

My role

Campaign setup, audience targeting, budget management, monitoring and optimisation. I also supported the creative work.

What I did

Used broad targeting for reach and purchase volume. For remarketing, I worked with website visitors, cart and checkout audiences, excluded recent purchasers and used product-focused messaging.

Broad: 161 purchases and 4.69× ROAS. Remarketing: 6.34× ROAS. These figures are shown by audience segment, not as a combined campaign total.

03Purchase-focused search optimisationGoogle Ads · Ecommerce · January–April 2026+

Challenge

Mixed conversion goals and lower-intent keywords made purchase-focused bidding harder.

My role

Campaign analysis and optimisation, including conversion goal review, keyword refinement and ad asset improvements.

What I changed

Shifted optimisation toward purchases, paused non-converting keywords, added negatives and higher-intent terms, and improved ad relevance.

Reported result

A$10,605.47 spend · A$70,265.24 conversion value · 6.6× ROAS.

The account also recorded 4,467 total conversions; that figure is not a purchase count.

What I can do in an account.

01 / DIAGNOSE

Find the reason an account feels hard to scale before making more changes.

  • Compare spend, CPA and ROAS with the target; identify audience, creative and landing page gaps.

Output: A prioritised list of what to fix, test, pause or leave alone.

02 / RUN

Campaign Build & Optimisation

Set up and manage campaigns with clear goals, clean tracking and deliberate budget decisions.

  • Build and QA Meta prospecting and retargeting campaigns, budgets, audiences, URLs and tracking.
  • Work with Google Search and Performance Max; review search intent, goals and ad relevance.

Output: A campaign setup and review rhythm tied to purchases and cost targets.

03 / TEST

Creative Strategy & Testing

Turn customer questions and campaign signals into useful ad ideas rather than another batch of random variations.

  • Research buyer concerns, offer and competitor messaging; plan angles, hooks and formats.
  • Brief static or video concepts, write copy, test one key variable and review hook, CTR, add-to-cart and purchase signals.

Output: A clear test plan and the next creative iteration.

Measurement & Reporting

Make the next decision from the numbers that matter to the business.

  • Read platform results alongside ecommerce orders and GA4, accounting for attribution differences.
  • Report on spend, CPA, ROAS and the funnel signals behind a recommendation.

Output: A concise account update with actions and the reason for each one.

Independent strategy sample

From customer friction to a creative test.

For a pet hair remover concept, I started with a familiar complaint: the room looks clean after vacuuming, but hair is still caught in the fabric. That gives the ad a specific problem to demonstrate.

The idea / “The second clean”

Show what the vacuum left behind.

Use the same close-up demonstration of hair lifted from a sofa or rug. The visual proves the problem quickly; the opening line is the variable.

Proposed A/B hook test

Two ways into the same demo.

A / Question

“You vacuumed. So why is pet hair still trapped?”

B / Observation

“The pet hair your vacuum leaves behind.”

Hold constant

Demo footage, offer, format, landing page and audience. Change the opening hook only.

Read the signals

Compare early video hold and click-through, then add-to-cart and purchases. If attention rises without purchase intent, check the page and offer before scaling.

Independent strategy sample, not a live campaign case study. The two hooks are proposed variations; no test outcome or sales result is claimed.
